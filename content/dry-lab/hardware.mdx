<Section id="overview" title="Hardware overview" kicker="01">

AURA's physical layer is a lateral-flow style strip and, for fluorescent reporters, a simple reader module. The goal is parlour-friendly: minimal parts, obvious readout, robust to temperature variation within reason.

</Section>

<Section id="strip" title="Diagnostic strip" kicker="02">

The strip has a sample well, capillary membrane, control line, and test line. Control line appearance is required for a valid test — same logic as commercial LFAs.

<StripDemo result="positive" />

**Design choices.**
- Control line always visible when flow works
- Test line colour chosen for contrast under farm lighting
- Housing protects membrane from handling damage

</Section>

<Section id="reader" title="Fluorescence reader" kicker="03">

For fluorescent reporters, a low-cost LED excitation and filtered photodiode module estimates signal intensity. The reader is optional if colorimetric output is sufficient.

<Section id="env" title="Environmental robustness" kicker="04">

Bench tests include modest temperature variation (e.g. 15–30 °C) because parlours are not climate-controlled labs. Results feed back into incubation time recommendations.

</Section>

<Section id="integration" title="Integration with workflow" kicker="05">

Hardware design followed stakeholder input: test at milking, result in minutes, no laptop required for basic interpretation. A phone photo archive is optional for record-keeping, not required for use.

</Section>
